AI makes intelligence cheaper. I think the market shifts from finding information to finding verifiable, proprietary knowledge on demand.

01 / The centerpiece

I want to start with Jevons Paradox.

When a resource becomes more efficient, we can end up consuming more of it, not less.

So if intelligence becomes dramatically cheaper, the number of questions worth asking explodes.

04 / The real question

What knowledge becomes defensible?

Public + explicit

AI increasingly makes this abundant. Great for discovery. Harder to preserve pricing power.

Proprietary + useful

Research, code, datasets, operating know-how. Valuable precisely because it is not freely available.

Local + contextual

Knowledge that is inside people and networks. Hard to crawl. Hard to standardize. Potentially very valuable.

For the end user

For research companies

11 / This is where licensing matters

Licensing creates repeatable cash flows.

Once you have repeatable licensable cash flows, the asset becomes easier to price, compare and eventually finance.

That is the bridge from a research marketplace to something much larger.

Start with the marketplace idea. Keep the exchange idea as the long-term option.

GMV

Total value of knowledge transactions flowing through FairFetch.

Take rate

A percentage of the licensing GMV created by matching demand and supply.

Enterprise/API

Governance, integration, audit, identity, budgets and agent access.

I want to be able to show what the GMV could become over ten years, then apply a take rate and understand what this company can be worth.

17 / The part I am still hung upon

Motion to market fit.

1. Reports first

Create decision-grade reports with verifiable information for a narrow consultant use case. Learn what buyers repeatedly ask for.

2. Agent access

Turn the repeated research into machine-readable assets that enterprise agents can discover and license.

3. Marketplace

Open supply once demand is legible. Standardize rights, provenance, pricing and settlement.

The marketplace is how this scales. It is probably not how this starts.
